# House-bank bus bar board: requirements

The bus bars, fuses, breakers and switches beside the house bank, built on
the bench and mounted in the under-berth beside the batteries. The drawing is
`house-bank-busbar-board.svg`, generated by `house-bank-busbar-board.py`.

The keywords MUST, SHALL, SHOULD and MAY are used as in RFC 2119.

## Boards

- B1. The positive side SHALL be two boards: a battery board for the Class T
  and the battery fuses, and a distribution board for the rest. The
  distribution board SHOULD come out of one 12 × 18 in HDPE sheet.
- B2. The positive board MAY be widened or trimmed. It SHOULD stay narrow,
  giving up the 12 in dimension before the 18 in one.
- B3. Splitting the assembly across boards SHOULD NOT force a complicated
  pattern; the layout stays simple.
- B4. The two positive boards SHALL butt edge to edge, or with a small gap,
  so they sit level; neither overlaps the other.

## Bars

- R1. Bus bars SHALL be 25 mm wide copper flat bar, cut and drilled by hand.
  Links to fuse holders MAY use narrower copper bar stock.
- R2. The positive bars SHALL sit on the left and the negative bars on the
  right, at similar heights, with a small gap between them.
- R3. That gap SHALL take a plastic divider a few millimetres thick.

## Batteries

- C1. The battery bars SHALL take four batteries. Two are fitted; the other
  two are a future expansion.
- C2. The batteries SHALL connect from below, and the board SHALL be placed
  so that each battery's cable, about 1 ft, reaches its bar.
- C3. On the positive board, the battery bar SHALL be on the right and
  everything else to its left.
- C4. Each battery SHALL land on both battery bars in its physical order,
  left to right, so every battery's leads run straight to its own studs. No
  battery's lead crosses another's, and the order on the positive bar and the
  negative bar is the same.
- C4a. Every battery SHALL see the same length of bar: the bar from its
  positive stud to the positive takeoff, plus the bar from its negative stud
  to the negative takeoff, SHALL be equal for all four, so the bank stays
  balanced. Balance comes from where the takeoffs sit, never from reordering
  the batteries (C4). With both takeoffs at the same end, the battery nearest
  them carries the most current on every charge and discharge and ages first.
- C4b. Any arrangement that meets C4 and C4a MAY be used. Those that change
  only the negative side, the easiest:
  - the shunt at the end of the negative battery bar opposite the positive
    takeoff, so the two takeoffs sit at diagonally opposite corners;
  - the shunt kept at the same end as the positive takeoff, with the negative
    battery bar fed from its far end by a return strap or second bar running
    back to the shunt;

  or, changing the positive side instead, the Class T moved to the end of the
  positive battery bar opposite the shunt.
- C5. Every battery's cables SHALL be the same length and gauge as every
  other battery's, and every battery fuse the same type, for the same reason.
- C6. Each battery SHALL have its own fuse between it and the battery bar.
  That fuse SHOULD be an ANL.
- C7. The cable from each battery to its fuse SHOULD be short, with no fuse
  on the battery terminal itself.
- C8. The per-battery fuses SHOULD hang down from the battery bar. The Class T
  MAY turn vertical, parallel to them, where that saves space.

## Protection and switching

- P1. A Class T fuse SHALL be the only path from the battery bar to any other
  positive bar.
- P2. The battery master switch SHALL be on this board, and only the house
  breaker SHALL be behind it.
- P3. Solar, the DC-DC charger, the inverter/charger, the second charger, the
  Cerbo GX, and the feed to the always-on fuses in the electrical panel SHALL
  bypass the master switch.
- P4. The inverter/charger SHALL have a service disconnect switch.
- P5. The master switch and the inverter service disconnect SHOULD sit on
  the left, where they are reached without lifting more than one berth
  board. A breaker MAY stand between a switch and the left edge.
- P6. Disconnects, fuses and outside connections SHOULD have their end points
  along the left and bottom edges of the board where reasonable.

- P7. What a hand operates, the switches and the breakers, SHOULD stand
  together at the left, so that no live bar is reached across.
- P8. The unswitched bar SHOULD carry one spare hole for a future load.

- P9. The inverter/charger feed SHOULD have a breaker after its service
  disconnect, as well as its fuse.
- P10. Breakers SHOULD lie with the input on the right and the lead leaving
  the left edge.

## Negative side

- N1. The SmartShunt SHALL be the only bridge between the negative battery
  bar and the negative distribution bar.
- N2. Only battery negatives SHALL land on the battery side of the shunt.
  Every other negative, the engine block included, SHALL land on the
  distribution side.

## Out of scope

- The always-on fuse bank lives in the electrical panel, not on this board.
- The Cerbo GX is fused inline on its feeder wire, not on this board.
- Ratings, ampacity and wire sizing are out of scope for the layout. A
  serious problem with any of them is still raised when it shows.
